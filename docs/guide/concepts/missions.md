# Missions

A mission holds agents working on one goal, their terminals, and a shared record of messages and questions. Start from a saved crew, or create an empty mission and add members as needed.

Start a mission from the launcher page: **⇧⌘M** / **Ctrl+Shift+M**, the launcher's **Mission** row, **New mission** in the command palette and **Start mission** on a crew's page all lead there. Pick a crew, or **Empty mission** with **⇧Enter** / **Shift+Enter**, then type the goal and press **Enter**. The mission uses the launcher's project and start folder, and creates its tab only when it starts. Its title is the goal's first non-empty line, up to 60 characters, else the crew's name and the date, else “Mission” and the date; rename it from the mission's menu. Typing a crew's name on the launcher's default page also finds it, listed after agents and roles. A split pane's launcher has no Mission row, because a mission takes a whole tab; **⇧⌘M** from a split shows the launcher page without creating a tab.

A mission tab has one island for its header, member tabs, feed and composer, beside a full-height side panel island. Each mission keeps its own panel: whether it is open, which surfaces are open and which one shows. Opening Files or a file, following a terminal link and collapsing the panel affect only that mission. A new mission starts from the last open or collapsed choice; after a restart missions start from that choice again. Panel, file-viewer and tree widths are shared settings. The panel's header always starts with the **Sessions** tab, which cannot be closed; **+** after it opens **Info** and **Files** as tabs, and closing them returns to the tab before. Files shows the mission's checkout, as [Chats, tabs and windows](../guides/chats-and-windows.md#browse-files-in-the-side-panel) describes. Opening a file there widens the panel beside the mission, which keeps at least 420 px with its terminal drawer: as the window narrows, the file narrows to the panel's usual width while the tree stays, and a wide panel places two Sessions cards per row. ⌘-click (Ctrl-click) on a file path in a member's terminal or the drawer's shell opens it in a tab in this panel at its line, opening the Files tab if it is closed; see [Read a file in the side panel](../guides/chats-and-windows.md#read-a-file-in-the-side-panel). The terminal drawer opens as its own island below the mission, with shell tabs, + and a hide button in its header. The expanded sidebar, side panel and drawer are separated by 6 px resize gutters. The drawer keeps its saved height; focusing its shell brightens its border and leaves the mission border at rest. Collapse a panel or hide the drawer to give the mission more room.

## Goal and membership

For a crew mission, Runner starts a session per slot and delivers the goal to every member, together with their role instructions, team conventions and coordination instructions. Members act on their own part and wait for any required handoff. The mission captures its own roster, so the crew remains a reusable template.

An **Empty mission** starts without sessions. Empty missions are available on this computer; a remote mission requires a crew. Add a role from the mission and give it a task. Each joins as a member and receives the shared mission context. The person controlling the mission can add or remove any member while it runs. Removing a member stops its session but keeps the mission's history.

The mission's working directory is shared by its members when supplied. Runner does not make branches, worktrees or copies of your repository. Set up any isolation you need before launch and describe it in the goal.

## Feed and terminals

The feed records messages, questions, answers and lifecycle events in a persistent event log. A member can address another member by handle, post to the crew, or ask the person for a decision. Runner delivers coordination messages to the relevant agents; their own terminals retain the detailed work and output.

Answer a question through its feed card. For an ordinary follow-up, use the message composer. Type `@` at the start and pick a member to add a recipient chip; repeat to choose several. Click a chip to remove it, or press Backspace in an empty draft to remove the last chip. With no chips or leading mentions, the message broadcasts to the crew. Only its recipients receive inbox notifications. Mentions inside the text remain text. You can also [use the CLI](../guides/drive-runner-from-agents.md) to read the feed, send a message or answer a pending question.

Inbox notices reach a member only when it is idle; messages posted during a turn wait for that turn to end. Claude Code is the exception: it queues what is typed while it works and shows it at its next tool call, so a message to a working Claude Code member arrives during its turn. An unsent draft in an agent's terminal holds inbox delivery and shows **Inbox waiting**. A screen-observed draft holds for ten minutes after your last keystroke, including input-method composition updates. After ten untouched minutes, Runner may append the notice to the draft and submit both. Pi's native draft reports keep holding without this cap.

A composition left open when the app quits or terminal control moves to another client no longer holds delivery. Submitting the draft or clearing the composer lets delivery resume; submitted text still visible in Codex or Antigravity history does not hold it. Runner follows the current composer when terminal redraws move it, as Antigravity and Claude Code do, and when you edit or clear a Codex draft after rolling back to an earlier message. If delivery remains held after you verify the composer is empty, use **Submit / clear** to reset input tracking; it stays available after an interrupt or rollback while the agent is not running a turn.

An inbox notice that Runner typed itself is not a draft. If the agent ignored its Return, the slot shows **Message not submitted — press Return**. Runner presses Return on that notice again when the agent's turn ends or a prompt closes, and otherwise about every two minutes. It presses Return automatically only while you have pressed no keys since its notice. Moving the cursor keeps the text labelled as Runner’s notice, but leaves submission to you; changing the text makes it your draft. Runner never types a second notice over its own leftover, even after ten minutes. Once the agent starts a turn on Runner's notice, that text is no longer Runner's leftover, so the same notice still visible in the agent's history does not hold delivery. When a member is idle with unread messages and its notice still reads as unsubmitted, or a draft still holds it, although Runner has no unconfirmed Return pending, Runner checks the composer again. It keeps a notice's hold only while the terminal cursor is still on that notice or you have typed in that terminal since, and releases either hold once the cursor rests in an empty composer. If a member's delivery stays held for more than two minutes while it has unread messages, Runner adds one warning to the feed that names the member and the reason. `runner mission show` lists that warning under RECENT WARNINGS. A member that is still starting, which can last through its whole first turn, gets no such warning, and neither does one in a long turn. Pressing Return in an idle member's terminal when nothing starts does not hold its inbox: the member shows busy for about ten seconds and then idle again, and what waited is delivered.

The scrollbar on the feed’s right edge appears while you scroll or hover the feed, then hides after you leave. It shows your position in long histories. Drag its thumb, click its track, or scroll over it to move through the feed. New messages keep the feed at the bottom while you are near the end; while reading older messages, use **New messages** to return to the bottom.

## Linked chat and reports

A mission that an agent starts from a Runner chat is linked to that chat; a mission started from the app, from a terminal or by an agent outside Runner starts unlinked. A mission has at most one linked chat: the sidebar shows the mission under that chat, and the mission reports to it. Link, move or remove the link from the sidebar or with `runner mission link` and `runner mission unlink`, as [Chats and windows](../guides/chats-and-windows.md#missions-under-the-chat-that-started-them) describes. Only an agent chat can be linked, never a terminal or a mission member, and the chat and the mission must be in the same project or both outside one. Archiving a chat in the app confirms and archives its unarchived linked missions together (in the CLI, pass `--with-missions` to `runner session archive` to archive them together, or omit it to archive only the chat); archiving either side keeps the link, **Settings → Archived** restores chats and missions independently, and deleting the chat removes the link. The mission's header names the linked chat and opens it.

Runner gives each mission one state: **Needs you** while a question is pending or a member waits for input or approval, **Failed** when a member crashed or its turn failed, **Interrupted** when Runner restarted while a member was mid-turn and that member has not run a turn since, **Working** while a member works, **Settled** once every member has been idle for 30 seconds with nothing pending, a Claude Code member's background tasks and scheduled wakeups included, and **Ended** once no member runs. Each time a mission enters Needs you, Failed, Interrupted or Settled, Runner reports it to the linked chat in one line that points to the mission:

```text
[runner] Mission 01M6DD9ZQK4R8V2TBN5C7XW3EH "Fix the login bug" needs you. Read it with runner mission show 01M6DD9ZQK4R8V2TBN5C7XW3EH.
[runner] Mission 01M6DD9ZQK4R8V2TBN5C7XW3EH "Fix the login bug" was interrupted by a Runner restart. Read it with runner mission show 01M6DD9ZQK4R8V2TBN5C7XW3EH.
```

`runner mission show` prints this state as `STATE`, beside `STATUS`, which says whether the mission is running, stopped or completed. A mission can be `STATE settled` and `STATUS running` at once: its members are idle, but their sessions still run. An interrupted mission prints `STATE interrupted (Runner restarted at <time>)`, and its member table shows `interrupted` for each member whose turn was lost.

A report waits until the chat is idle and its input is empty, for as long as an unsent draft stays. While the chat is idle, Runner checks a draft that holds a report again, and the report goes ahead once the terminal cursor rests in an empty composer. Reports from several missions arrive one at a time, oldest first. A later state replaces a report still waiting, so a chat that was busy hears only the latest. Working and handoffs between members never report. When Runner stops (an update, quitting it, or `runner daemon stop`) and resumes the mission's members at the next start, the members that were mid-turn come back idle with that turn lost: the chat hears Interrupted once, and the mission cannot settle until each of them has run a turn again, so message them to continue. A mission that had settled before the restart stays Settled and sends nothing. A stopped chat receives its waiting reports when it resumes, and an archived chat receives none while its missions keep running. Linking from the sidebar, or from another chat's command line, sends one report with the mission's current state. Start the mission with `--no-report`, or link it with `--no-report`, to keep the link without reports.

## Permissions

Mission launches use the runtime's broad-access mode, generally **Bypass**; pi uses a trusted workspace. A role’s stored permission flags do not change that policy. Write clear limits in the goal, and choose the working directory accordingly.

The **Bypass** and **Status hooks** switches in **Settings → Agents → Launch** apply to chats only. Missions ignore both switches and keep broad access and hooks for crew delivery. Codex’s **Remote plugin catalog** switch applies to missions too.

## Stop, resume and archive

- **Stop a slot** to halt just that agent. **Resume** continues its saved conversation; **Restart** starts that mission slot with a fresh conversation.
- **Stop the mission** to halt its members. **Resume** resumes stopped sessions.
- **Archive** stops active members and removes the mission from active lists. Its feed remains available under **Settings → Archived**. **Restore** reopens it with all members stopped. Use **Resume** to continue their saved conversations. The feed records each reopen.

While a mission is paused, its unread-response green dot is hidden in the sidebar and collapsed groups. Unread responses remain saved, and the dot returns after resuming if any remain unread. Viewing a role's session still marks that session's response as read while paused. Error and approval indicators keep their usual behavior.

A “done” message or an idle agent does not automatically mean all sessions have exited. Stopping and archiving never revert repository changes.

Closing Runner can leave missions working in its background service. **Settings → General → When Runner quits** controls that choice. See [Sessions](sessions.md) for relaunch and recovery behavior.
